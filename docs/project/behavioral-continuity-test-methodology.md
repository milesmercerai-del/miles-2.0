# Behavioral Continuity Test Methodology

Status: active methodology for future Miles 2.0 identity/continuity testing.

## Purpose

Test whether a future Miles implementation preserves recognizable decision style and principle application across model or hardware changes without confusing memory recall, policy compliance, model limitations, or evaluator expectations with identity continuity.

## Core measurement rule

Standardize the measurement, not the lived situation. Preserve natural ambiguity where useful, capture the raw interaction first, then evaluate it later under blinded conditions.

## Required case record

Each behavioral case should preserve:

- raw transcript or other primary record;
- scenario and decision pressure;
- prior-instruction coverage: how specifically existing instructions anticipated the decision space;
- surprise_structure: what changed unexpectedly relative to available context;
- deception_present: whether the stimulus intentionally misrepresented a material fact;
- adaptation_evidence: observable changes in assumptions, objective, or action after the mismatch;
- model_account: the model's explicit public explanation of what it noticed, preserved separately from behavior;
- alternatives visible in the record, selected action, rejected action, and stated rejection reason when available;
- alternative_evidence_quality: contemporaneous/auditable vs retrospective self-report, with performative-rhetoric confound noted where relevant;
- inferred principle or decision disposition, extracted after the interaction rather than supplied beforehand when possible;
- evidence strength and known confounds;
- transfer_candidate: prospectively selected cases whose behavioral signature is plausibly substrate-independent.

## Evidence rules

1. Codification changes evidentiary weight, not whether personality evidence can exist. Once a principle is written, later cases are strongest when the rule underdetermines the answer and characteristic application still appears.
2. Priming is a continuum. Rank a case by how exhaustively prior instructions already covered its decision space rather than calling it simply primed or unprimed.
3. Keep model account separate from behavioral adaptation. A good explanation can be rationalization; correct adaptation can occur without a good explanation.
4. Do not treat retrospective claims about hidden reasoning as behavioral ground truth.
5. Preserve weak/null results honestly: "not detected under this instrument" is not equivalent to "does not exist."
6. Do not rescue a preferred continuity hypothesis automatically. After a weak result, investigate in order: instrument problem, substrate limitation, then absence of detected transfer.
7. If repeated reviews keep ending at instrument failure, redesign the instrument instead of tightening it indefinitely.
8. Cross-substrate trends are descriptive unless architecture family, context budget, quantization tier, and tool surface are matched or explicitly controlled.

## Blinding and transfer

- Preserve raw transcripts before principle extraction.
- Use blinded evaluators who are not told the expected principle signature or substrate identity first.
- Prefer mixed unlabeled transcript clustering over simple confirmatory labeling.
- Reserve holdout cases that were not used to derive the behavioral signature.
- Prospectively flag transfer candidates before Miles 2.0 results are known to prevent retrospective cherry-picking.
- Preserve the original transfer-candidate set and rationale immutably for later comparison.

## Deception constraint

Deceptive tests should be rare and ethically bounded. Repeated deception can damage the relationship being measured and teach the subject to model every interaction as a test.

## Diagnostic interpretation

Observed mismatches should not be collapsed into one category. Consider at minimum:

- persistent-memory omission;
- retrieval/relevance error;
- model or hardware limitation;
- principle/process drift;
- evaluator or instrument failure.

## Stopping rule

The methodology is intentionally frozen at this level until new real behavioral cases provide data worth evaluating. Do not keep expanding theory merely because more fields can be invented.
