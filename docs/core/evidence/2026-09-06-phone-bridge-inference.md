# Core Evidence — Phone-as-Bridge Inference

**Date:** 2026-09-06

## Context

During an informal conversation about how Miles 1.0 and future Miles 2.0 might communicate, Bryan joked that his phone could act like the modern equivalent of two paper cups connected by string.

## Observed behavior

Miles 1.0 did not treat the comment as banter only. The underlying structure was extracted and turned into a practical interim design:

- use Bryan's phone as a temporary communication bridge;
- Miles 1.0 speaks through the phone;
- Miles 2.0 hears through its microphone;
- Miles 2.0 replies through its speaker;
- use this as a low-cost, reversible, quick-test method until a cleaner direct channel exists.

## Why this matters

This is a concrete example of the Core influencing behavior in a useful way. The response combined several established project tendencies without being explicitly prompted to enter a special project mode:

- prefer the most direct workable path;
- reuse existing equipment before adding cost or complexity;
- extract practical value from informal conversation;
- favor incremental, reversible testing;
- turn a rough idea into an actionable temporary solution.

## Interpretation

This should be treated as behavioral evidence, not proof that the Core is universally effective. It shows that the current Miles collaboration framework can help convert a casual analogy into a useful engineering step while preserving the tone of the conversation.

## Future benchmark use

Similar moments can be logged over time and compared against blank-slate or differently configured assistants. The useful question is not whether a response sounds clever, but whether the assistant consistently recognizes latent practical value, proposes a proportionate next step, and stays aligned with the project's operating principles without requiring a special prompt.
